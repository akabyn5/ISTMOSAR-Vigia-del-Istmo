    04 / SCIENCE & METHODOLOGY

    Radar measurements, not photographs.

    What the previews show, how they are made, and what they cannot tell you. The map shows exploratory image previews, not validated analysis results.

    01

    NISAR and L-band SAR

    NISAR is a joint NASA–ISRO Earth-observation radar mission. Its L-band synthetic aperture radar (SAR) is an active microwave instrument: it transmits pulses and records the energy scattered back, called backscatter. The return depends on surface roughness, moisture, vegetation structure and viewing geometry. It is not a photograph.

    02

    Level-2 GCOV

    GCOV (Geocoded Polarimetric Covariance) is a Level-2 product on a map grid, here UTM zone 17N at 10 m pixels. Backscatter is gamma-zero linear power with terrain-related radiometric correction. Supporting layers include quality-related masks and number of looks; the masks are not applied to these previews.

    03

    HHHH and HVHV

    HHHH is the co-polarized diagonal covariance term (horizontal transmit, horizontal receive); HVHV is the cross-polarized term. They are real-valued, distinct measurements that respond to different scattering and must not be treated as interchangeable. The previews show HHHH only.

    04

    Provisional data

    The Composite Release ID (CRID) of all three granules, P05023, is the one ASF documents for PROVISIONAL NISAR products. Provisional products are calibrated and partially validated; quality may not be optimal, and validated reprocessing may supersede them. The raw CMR UMM-G records for all three selected granules have been retrieved and parsed. Their granule concept IDs and GranuleUR values match the corresponding project granule records. Each record identifies the NISAR_L2_GCOV_PROVISIONAL_V1 collection, Version 1. The exported CMR spatial-footprint GeoJSON for each scene also matches the spatial boundary in its UMM-G record. This verifies catalogue provenance and footprint-record consistency; it does not approve the project study area, pairwise scientific compatibility, QA policy, or environmental interpretation.

    RADIOMETRIC DISPLAY

    Linear power to decibels

    GCOV values are linear power. Positive values are converted to decibels with the power conversion shown here; the factor is 10 because these are power values (20 would be for amplitude). NoData and values that are non-positive after resampling have no dB value and are shown transparent. A full-scene scan of the three Stage 2 HHHH rasters found no finite zero or negative source values and no positive or negative infinities. NaN/NoData samples remain. The previews average-resample linear power before converting positive destination values to dB. However, the treatment and spatial influence of source NaN/NoData samples during average resampling have not been independently quantified. This remains an exploratory display workflow, not a validated quality-control procedure.

    γ⁰dB = 10 log10(γ⁰linear)
    PREVIEW METHOD

    How the previews are made

    1. Read the Stage 2 Frequency A HHHH GeoTIFF in gamma-zero linear power.
    2. Reproject to Web Mercator (EPSG:3857) and average-resample the linear-power values to 1800 × 1773 pixels.
    3. Convert the positive resampled values to dB. Non-positive resampled values are left transparent.
    4. Apply one shared grayscale stretch, −25.9 to −4.4 dB (2nd–98th percentile of the three previews), to every scene.
    5. Export lightweight transparent PNGs. Product quality masks are not applied.

    The averaging is done on linear power before the dB conversion. Previews are about 190 m per pixel, much coarser than the 10 m source data, and are not a substitute for the original measurements.

    PROVISIONAL EXTENT

    The dashed rectangle is not a study area

    The overlay and the dashed outline show the envelope of the shared raster grid transformed to latitude and longitude. It is a provisional visualization extent. It is not an acquisition footprint, not a watershed and not an approved study-area boundary. About half of each preview is transparent because the imaged swath does not fill the rectangle.

    Data quality limits: GCOV mask semantics have been reviewed against the applicable NISAR GCOV Product Specification, Revision F. Full-scene distributions of mask, inputDataExceptionMask, and numberOfLooks have also been inspected. The two mask layers have distinct meanings, and neither is applied to these exploratory previews. The nine candidate upstream QA sidecars previously examined were HTML responses, not valid PDF, HDF5, or CSV QA products. ASF sidecar enumeration/recovery and local nisarqa execution remain outstanding. No approved QA policy or common-valid-pixel mask exists. All quantitative date pairs remain pending. The June 16 scene has Forecast orbit and pointing fidelity; June 28 and October 14 are Near Real-Time.

Environmental interpretation requires independent evidence and validated processing.

    05 / ABOUT & DATA SOURCES

    Built in Panama by Space Dogs.

    ISTMOSAR is an ARPIP research and technology-development project within Space Dogs, a Panama-based student-led space technology group.

    Project intent

    Develop a lightweight public interface for exploring selected NISAR L-band observations and communicating the scientific workflow, data provenance, and limitations clearly.

    Implementation approach

    HTML, CSS, and JavaScript provide the static application. Leaflet supports the interactive map; OpenStreetMap supplies the basemap. Small JSON files describe the scenes and the provisional extent. The previews are lightweight PNG display products generated outside this repository from Stage 2 GeoTIFFs; the original HDF5 files and full-size GeoTIFFs are not stored on GitHub. Previews are exploratory and are not scientifically approved.
